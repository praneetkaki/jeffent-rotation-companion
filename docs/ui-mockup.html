The ear — anatomy to the clinic

External, middle, and inner ear; hearing loss, otitis, and the vertigo syndromes. Tagged for day-1 rotators and sub-I depth.

Middle ear & tympanic membrane

Start at the surface you actually see on otoscopy, then build inward to the ossicles and the facial nerve's course.

  • TM landmarks: cone of light, umbo, malleus handle, pars tensa/flaccida.
  • Ossicles: malleus → incus → stapes → oval window.
  • CN VII runs through the temporal bone — weakness with ear disease is a red flag.

Label practice

  • Umbo
  • Cone of light
  • Malleus handle

Hearing loss — the fork in the road

Conductive (cerumen, effusion, perforation, otosclerosis) vs sensorineural (presbycusis, noise, sudden SNHL, retrocochlear). Weber + Rinne localize it at the bedside.

⚑ Red flags in this module

⚑Sudden SNHL (<72h) — emergency: urgent audiogram, steroids, MRI.
⚑Asymmetric SNHL / unilateral tinnitus — MRI for vestibular schwannoma.
⚑Facial weakness + ear disease — urgent ENT.

Work the case

A 34-year-old has one day of muffled hearing and new ringing on the right. No wax. Weber lateralizes left; Rinne positive bilaterally.
Teaching point: a sensorineural pattern = sudden SNHL, an emergency — not "just wax." Urgent audiogram + steroids.
